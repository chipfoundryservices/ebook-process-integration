# Preface: The Hidden Precision Problem

Semiconductor manufacturing looks like a problem of raw power—how much plasma energy do we need to etch deeper, faster, with higher selectivity? But the real constraint is not energy. It is *uniformity at scale*.

A single 300mm wafer has 10 million transistor sites. An advanced node like 3nm packs them into a 100mm² die. A fab runs 50-100 such chambers. Each chamber processes 100+ wafers per day. Across a year, a single fab performs trillions of etching operations.

If selectivity drifts by 5% across one chamber, one fab might lose 1% of wafer yield—a $500k loss per day. If it drifts 5% across the fleet, the fab can lose 5% of yield—$2.5M per day, or nearly $1B annually. 

This is why process integration engineers command $200k+ salaries and equipment suppliers invest $50M+ annually in process development.

This book covers the engineering that prevents that drift—and the economics of manufacturers who master it.
